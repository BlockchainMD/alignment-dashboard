Sources & collection coverage

New research is checked hourly while historical discovery continues in the background. “Available” means a source responded; it does not mean every article has been collected. Feed windows, backfill progress, failures, and warnings are shown below.

An archive that keeps its history

Records are retained without a rolling expiry. Dedicated alignment sources are collected broadly; uncertain matches from mixed sources remain visible as candidates. Keyword labels organize the collection and are not assessments of quality. Publication dates stay unknown when a source does not provide them.

Original links, identifiers, author metadata, and source provenance are preserved when available. Abstracts and excerpts are source text; aggregator summaries are labeled. Full articles are not mirrored here.

Coverage is bounded by the configured sources and queries. This is not a guarantee of all alignment research published anywhere. Historical backfill is gradual and some sources expose only a recent feed window.

Collection is scheduled at 17 minutes past every hour. GitHub scheduling is best effort and can be delayed or skipped. A warning appears after three hours without a successful collection. “Reload” fetches the latest published archive.

Next: research into short explainers

The archive preserves stable article IDs and source metadata for a future video pipeline. Research packets can be exported from the repository. No videos or social posts are generated automatically.

The intended flow is source verification → plain-language script → claim and limitation review → narration and visuals → a rendered duration check of 120 seconds or less → publishing review. A packet is an input for this process, not a verified script.
